THE CLASSROOM

Study with a purpose.

Start the week by checking your course materials and writing down the next deadlines. Break each assignment into a first step you can finish in one sitting. For a finance problem set, that might mean reviewing one worked example and attempting three questions before checking the solutions.

For a study group, agree on the topic before meeting. Ask each person to bring a question they have already attempted. Spend the session explaining the reasoning behind your answers, then leave with a short list of what still needs practice.

Try this week

Book two focused study sessions and one review session. Give each a clear task instead of simply writing “study” on your calendar.

WHAT COMES NEXT

Make recruiting a routine.

Recruiting is easier to manage when it has a regular place in your schedule. Keep a simple list of target roles with application links, deadlines and next steps. Pick a small number of opportunities you can research well rather than treating every opening the same way.

Set aside separate time for outreach and interview preparation. Practice explaining one class project in terms of the problem, your contribution and the result. For official appointments, recruiting events and program guidance, use the resources provided by SMU and your graduate program.

Try this week

Research one employer, improve one resume bullet and prepare a short introduction. Follow up on a conversation with a specific reason to reconnect.

THE PEOPLE AROUND YOU

Build connections that last.

You do not need a formal event to get to know someone. Invite a classmate for coffee after class or offer to compare approaches to a challenging assignment. Small, regular conversations can turn into stronger study partnerships and a more supportive graduate experience.

Leave some unscheduled time in the week. A plan that uses every free hour can be difficult to maintain when deadlines change. Protect time for rest and the activities that help you return to class focused.

Try this week

Reach out to a classmate you have not worked with yet. Suggest one specific time and a simple activity, such as lunch or a short review session.
